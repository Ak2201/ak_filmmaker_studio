Switching from Celtx

What the studio reads, what the move looks like and what comes out again. Everything here is about {{brand:name}}; for what Celtx can save, check Celtx's own help.

What the studio reads

The Screenplay Writer's Import Screenplay reads these files. Each is read in your browser; nothing is uploaded to read it.

  • Final Draft (.fdx). Every line's type is already in the file, so nothing is guessed, and a locked scene number such as 12A comes across as it is.
  • Fountain (.fountain or .spmd), the plain-text screenplay format.
  • Word (.docx). Lines are placed by their screenplay style names (Scene Heading, Character and so on), or by their indents when the file has no such styles.
  • Plain text (.txt), laid out as a screenplay or written as Fountain.
  • A PDF with real text in it. A scanned or password-protected PDF is refused with the reason, rather than read wrongly.
  • Or paste the screenplay's text straight into the import window.

The move, step by step

  1. Save a copy from your current tool. Save or export your script in one of the formats above. Check your tool's own help for which of them it can write; a Final Draft (.fdx) file carries the most, because every line's type is in it.
  2. Open Import Screenplay. In a project of your own, open the Screenplay Writer and choose File, then Import Screenplay. Choose the file, or paste the text.
  3. Read the preview. Before anything is written, the import says how it read the file: as which format, and how many lines, pages and scenes it found.
  4. Choose where it goes. If the screenplay already has pages, choose to add the import after them or to replace them. Replacing saves a draft of what was there first, and the old scene rows go to the scene bin rather than being deleted.
  5. Carry on from the scenes. The script's scene headings become the project's scene list, which the breakdown, the stripboard and the call sheets read, on the plans that include those tools.

What comes out again

The Screenplay Writer's File menu exports PDF, Word (.docx), Final Draft (.fdx) and Fountain. Export one straight after the import and open it in the app you came from: that is the quickest way to see what travelled.

A backup file holds every project in your studio and restores into any browser, and on Intermediate and Pro a backup can also be kept in your own Google Drive.

What it costs

Importing a screenplay and the Screenplay Writer are on every plan. A plan is a year of the studio. Checkout shows the currency, the exact amount you will be charged and the plan's terms before you pay.

Also in the studio

A few things the studio does once your script is in.

translate

Tamil typing in dialogue

Type Tamil in Roman letters and it is offered in Tamil script, inside dialogue and parentheticals only, so slug lines and action stay in English and the page count stays exact.

fact_check

Script to call sheet in one chain

Scene headings become scenes; the breakdown, stripboard, day out of days and call sheets all read those same scenes.

payments

A budget in rupees

A line-by-line estimate that starts from dated Chennai rate bands, takes its days from your schedule and tracks spending against the plan.

backup

Backup to your own Google Drive

On Intermediate and Pro, a backup file of your studio is written to your own Drive, which you can open, download or delete.
