# Blueprint realignment — plan

Status: PLAN, not built. Owner's ask (6 Oct 2026): "Story › Blueprint ›
Story, which also has Pre-Production, Production and Post-Production —
it will confuse the user. Realign them, make them alive, remove nothing."

## 1. What is confusing today

The app has two maps of the same journey, and one is filed inside the
other.

| Map | Where it lives | Its parts |
|---|---|---|
| **The five stages** (tools) | the phase bar, `navigation.json` `phases` | Story · Screenplay · Pre-Production · Production · Post-Production |
| **The Feature Blueprint** (a guide) | filed as a module of the **Story** stage | Phase 01 Story (steps 1–12) · Phase 02 Pre-Production (13–24) · Phase 03 Production (25–28) · Phase 04 Post-Production (29–32) |
| **The Short Blueprint** (a guide) | also under **Story** | 11 steps running from the seed to the festival plan, with no stages at all |

So a user clicks **Story › Feature Blueprint** and lands on a page that
says **Phase 02 · Pre-Production**: a stage nested inside a different
stage. Three more mismatches make it worse:

- The word "phase" is used for both the stages and the blueprint's
  four parts, and they disagree: four parts against five stages, and no
  Screenplay part at all. Steps 8–12 are screenplay work.
- The blueprint never says which tool does the job it describes. Step 16
  "Storyboard & Shot List" has no link to the Storyboard and Shot List
  modules, so the guide and the tools feel like two separate apps.
- The tools never point back to the guide.

## 2. The idea in one line

**The Blueprint is the guide that runs beside all five stages, not a
module inside one of them.** The stages are where you do the work; the
blueprint is how to think about it. Nothing moves out of either page.
Only where they are filed changes, along with what they are called and
how they link to each other.

## 3. The realignment (nothing removed)

### 3.1 Navigation

- **A new "Blueprints" shelf** in the rail, beside the Library, holding
  Feature Blueprint and Short Blueprint. `navmodel.js` already supports
  a global entry that carries modules (that is how the Library's three
  tabs work), so this needs no new machinery.
  - The **module ids stay** `feature-blueprint` and `short-blueprint`,
    so plan features keyed by id, the launcher's "N steps started"
    counts and the palette all keep working.
- **The Story stage keeps** Beats & Pacing and Idea Vault, its tools.
- **Each stage's menu gains a "Guide for this stage" footer row** that
  links into the matching part of each blueprint:
  - **Story:** feature steps 1–7 · short steps 1–5
  - **Screenplay:** feature steps 8–12 · short steps 6–8
  - **Pre-Production:** feature steps 13–24 · short step 9
  - **Production:** feature steps 25–28 · short step 9
  - **Post-Production:** feature steps 29–32 · short step 10

  It is a cross-reference in its own row, not a module, so the stage
  still lists only tools.
- **The breadcrumb on a blueprint** reads `Blueprints › Feature ›
  Part II · Pre-Production`, not `Story › Feature Blueprint`.

### 3.2 The blueprint's own words

- **"Phase" becomes "Part"** inside the blueprint, so the word "phase"
  only ever means a stage of the app. Each part names the stages it
  covers:
  - Part I · Story & Screenplay (steps 1–12)
  - Part II · Pre-Production (13–24)
  - Part III · Production (25–28)
  - Part IV · Post-Production (29–32)
- **Every step gets a stage chip,** a small coloured tag in that stage's
  hue: "STORY", "SCREENPLAY", "PRE-PRODUCTION". The Short Blueprint's
  11 steps get the same chips, so it gains a visible structure without
  being reordered.
- **What does not change:**
  - The section ids `#vol-1`, `#vol-2`, `#phase-3` and `#phase-4`,
    because deep links and the jump menu use them.
  - Every `data-key`, including `v1_*`, `v2_*`, `p3_*` and `p4_*`.
  - The step order.
- **Where the stage mapping lives:** a sidecar,
  `src/data/steps.stages.json`, like `steps.priority.json`. The step
  files are regenerated by `npm run extract`, which would wipe anything
  written into them.

## 4. Making them alive

In build order; each part works on its own.

1. **"Do this in…" on every step.**
   - **What it does:** a row of buttons under the step's prompts that
     opens the tool where the work actually happens.
     - Step 08 *15 Beats* → Beats & Pacing · Write › Outline
     - Step 11 *Scene List* → Breakdown › Scene List
     - Step 15 *Lookbook* → Visualize › Lookbook
     - Step 16 → Shot List · Storyboard
     - Step 21 *Locations & Recce* → Plan › Locations
     - Step 23 *Schedule, Budget & Crew* → Stripboard · Budget · Contacts
     - Step 25 *Shoot Day* → Shoot Day · Call Sheets
     - Steps 29–30 → Edit Log
     - Step 32 *Delivery* → Deliverables
   - **Where the links come from:** the same sidecar, by module id, so a
     renamed page needs no edit here.
2. **Live readouts inside the steps.**
   - **What it does:** each step shows a line of what the film's tools
     already know.
     - Step 11: "Your breakdown has 36 scenes · 105 pages · 4 with no
       location".
     - Step 23: "18 shoot days scheduled · budget ₹… · 29 in the unit".
     - Step 25: "Day 3 of 18 · 6 scenes shot".
   - **How:** derived at render time from `readiness.js` and the scene
     model, and stored nowhere, by the same rule `readiness.js` follows.
     An empty film says "Nothing in the Breakdown yet", with the button.
3. **The guide inside each tool.**
   - **What it does:** a small "Blueprint step 16" pill on each module
     page. It opens a side drawer showing that step's question and the
     answer you already wrote there. The answer can be edited in place,
     and saves to the same `data-key`. You can think and do on one
     screen.
   - **How:** the drawer reuses the steps renderer. It writes through the
     existing save path, not a second copy of the field.
4. **The journey strip.**
   - **What it does:** one band on the hub and dashboard showing the five
     stages, in order. Each stage shows two numbers: guide progress
     (steps ticked) and tool progress (the readiness checks passed).
     The current stage is highlighted ("you are here"), with a "Next:
     Screenplay — open Write" call to action.
   - **How:** derived only; it adds no storage key.
5. **Hand-offs, opt-in and undoable.**
   - **What it does:** a button on a step that moves its answer into the
     matching tool.
     - Step 11's scene list → "Send these scenes to the Breakdown".
     - Step 2's logline → "Use as the Story synopsis's first line".
     - Step 15's references → "Add to Lookbook".
   - **Safety rules:**
     - Each hand-off is an explicit click with Undo, never automatic.
     - A hand-off never writes over existing work. It adds, as the
       script → breakdown hand-off already does.
6. **Stage completion moments.**
   - **What it does:** ticking the last check of a part shows a short
     wrap card, for example "Part I done: logline, 15 beats, 42 scenes",
     with the next part and its tools.
   - **How:** it is derived from the existing ticks and writes nothing
     new.

## 5. Order of work

| # | Work | Touches | Gate impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Navigation: the Blueprints shelf, the guide row, the breadcrumb, and the extension panel reading `moduleGroups()` (this also fixes KNOWN-ISSUES #2) | `navigation.json`, `navmodel.js`, `shell.js`, `panel.js` | hub and nav words move → deliberate re-baseline, **zero data-key movement** |
| B | Wording: Part I–IV, the stage chips, the sidecar | `feature.js` covers, `short.js`, `steps.js`, `steps.stages.json` | feature and short words move → re-baseline |
| C | "Do this in…" links | `steps.js`, sidecar | words only |
| D | Live readouts | `steps.js`, `readiness.js` (read only) | the seeded verify run sees new words; no keys |
| E | The guide pill and drawer in modules | new `src/ui/blueprint-drawer.js`, each module page | writes through the existing keys, proved by a round-trip check |
| F | The journey strip | hub, dashboard | derived only |
| G | Hand-offs and completion cards | `steps.js`, `scenes.js`, `story.js` | new prove script for add-only and Undo |

A to C are the realignment the owner asked for. D to G are what makes
the blueprints alive. Each letter can be one agent, merged in this order.

**Proof for every step:**
- `npm run verify` is green after each deliberate re-baseline, and each
  baseline diff shows no data-key movement.
- A browser check of the blueprint pages at 390px.
- The 18 fragment loads (`#vol-1`, `#phase-3`, …) still land on their
  sections.

## 6. Decisions for the owner

1. **Where the blueprints live.**
   - **Recommended:** their own "Blueprints" shelf in the rail, beside
     the Library.
   - The alternative is a tab inside the Library. That is fewer rail
     items, but it buries the main guided path inside a reference shelf.
2. **Part I's name.**
   - **Recommended:** "Story & Screenplay", one part with per-step chips.
   - The alternative is to split Part I into two parts (Story 1–7,
     Screenplay 8–12), giving five parts that match the five stages
     exactly. That changes the "Phase 01" cover into two covers.
3. **Whether the drawer (item E) may edit the answer, or only show it.**
   - **Recommended:** edit, through the same save path.
