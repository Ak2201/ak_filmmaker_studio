# Launch-reaction simulation: FilmMakerStudio, 21 Oct 2026

## 1. What this is

This is a simulated study, not market research. Ten AI personas, each playing a type of Tamil filmmaker or adjacent professional, read the same public launch brief. In round 1 each reacted on their own. In round 2 they read each other's posts and replied. Use it to find objections, unclear wording and the arguments that spread between people. It is not a sales forecast and says nothing reliable about conversion rates.

## 2. Headline

- **(A) The reaction is curious, but nobody trusts it yet.** 7 of 10 were curious on first read and 3 were skeptical. Nine said they would try it at some point. Nobody gave a firm "yes, I'd pay." Every persona called the price cheap, and none said price was the blocker.
- **(A) Every persona raised the same objection: the trial.** All 10 said the 30-minute trial on the sample films is too short to judge the product, and 7 named it as their top objection. The underlying complaint is that they can't try it on their own script. The second biggest objection is that nobody they know has used it on a real shoot or film (7 asked for that kind of proof).
- **(A) The specific gaps that came up:** anyone with a view link can see the budget, "local-first" doesn't sit well with Google sign-in, the 14-day offline limit and the device check-in, nobody knows whether FDX round-trips cleanly or keeps revision marks, and call sheets are Pro-only.
- **(B) The price rise mostly does nothing.** 6 of 10 said "no effect," 3 said it "feels like pressure, puts me off," and 1 (the indie director) said it "pushes me to buy now." At these prices people don't feel urgency. Where they notice the deadline at all, it is because a 30-minute trial sits next to a 10-day deadline, and that pairing reads as being rushed to buy before you can judge.
- **(B) The way the countdown is shown earned trust.** The influencer said a real count and a real date, rather than a fake timer, kept them from "calling it a scam."

## 5. Objections, ranked

1. **The trial is too short, runs only on the sample films, and won't take your own script.** 10 of 10 mention it; 7 call it their top objection.
   > "bro I can't even finish reading the sample breakdown in 30 mins 😭" (film-school student)

2. **No proof from someone they know, and an unknown team.** 7 asked for a peer they know to vouch for it (director, student, 1st AD, line producer, Celtx writer, YouTube maker, veteran). The OTT writer and the veteran also named the small, unknown team.
   > "Anyone actually used it on a real shoot?" (line producer)

3. **Export and import fidelity: does FDX round-trip, and do revision marks survive?** Raised by 5: Celtx writer, OTT writer, director, influencer and Bengaluru (in round 2). The OTT and Celtx writers specifically want revision colours and A/B pages.
   > "does a Celtx FDX import keep dual dialogue, scene numbers and revision marks?" (Celtx writer)

4. **Anyone with a view link can read the budget.** 2 in round 1 (line producer, veteran). By round 2 it was 4: the 1st AD and influencer took it up, and the AD tied it to sharing the project for call sheets.
   > "Naan budget-ah full crew kitta open-ah kudukka maaten." (line producer)

5. **"Local-first" doesn't match sign-in, sync on by default, the 14-day limit and the check-in.** 2 in round 1 (veteran, Bengaluru). The influencer agreed in round 2.
   > "Then it's not really local-first, right?" (Bengaluru tech-indie)

6. **The deadline feels like pressure.** 3 (1st AD, veteran, influencer).
   > "Second, the \"price goes up 31 Oct\" line: at least they show a real count (2/100) and a real date, not a fake timer, so I'm not calling it a scam. But I won't rush you either." (influencer)

7. **Where features sit in the tiers.** 3 personas. Call sheets are Pro-only (1st AD, YouTube maker), and own-key AI is Pro-only (Bengaluru).
   > "AI is \"own key\" but locked behind the Pro tier, so I'm paying for something I bring myself." (Bengaluru tech-indie)

8. **Writing on a phone.** 2 (YouTube maker; the student in round 2).
   > "Naan phone la dhaan script ezhudhuren, idhu phone browser la smooth ah work aagudha?" (YouTube maker)

9. **No free tier and no student price.** 2 (student, YouTube maker).

10. **One persona each:**
    - Not built for a writers' room (live co-editing, per-writer tracking, GST company billing, NDA terms): OTT writer.
    - No import from an existing Excel breakdown: 1st AD.
    - Most rates are the un-rechecked 2024-25 card: line producer.
    - Native Celtx import isn't mentioned: Celtx writer.

## 6. What people noticed

**Features that mattered:**
- **Stripboard → DOOD → call sheet → WhatsApp:** director, 1st AD, line producer and influencer. The YouTube maker called it "nice." This was the strongest pull for people who work in production.
- **Tamil/Tanglish writing with automatic formatting:** student, Celtx writer, YouTube maker, OTT writer, influencer, veteran ("handles Tamil properly").
- **Open exports (FDX, PDF, Word, Fountain, CSV):** Celtx writer, OTT writer, veteran, Bengaluru and the director (as protection against lock-in). In round 2, the director and Bengaluru used the exports to push back on the veteran.
- **Budget from Chennai rates, with each line's source and date:** director and line producer. The line producer valued the sourcing and also distrusted the stale card.
- **One payment a year, no auto-renew:** Bengaluru and influencer.
- **Own-key AI:** Bengaluru and influencer, both approving of the idea while questioning why it is Pro-only.
- **Shown in minutes, not seconds:** the influencer noticed the real count and date.

**Nobody mentioned:**
- The Story stage: the four steps, the eleven structures, pacing.
- Specific AI tools, including the simulated audience test. Only the influencer named coverage and the dialogue pass, in round 2.
- Storyboard, shot list and sides.
- Drive backup. Bengaluru listed it once in a price comparison.
- The screening-room pass, even though it partly answers the budget-visibility worry.
- The per-day prices (Rs 1.64 / 2.19 / 2.74).
- The credit for unused time when moving up mid-year.
- Remove branding.
- The support address and the two-working-day promise.

The home-page recording got one mention ("Video paathen, sample padathula nalla dhaan irukku," 1st AD). The refund policy came up only as "purchases are final" (director). The preloaded sample was mentioned only as a limitation, never as a plus.

## 7. Word of mouth

- **The influencer was the hub.** 8 of the 9 other personas replied to the tools influencer, and most of those replies were test requests: Tanglish import, a real 30–40-day board, budget CSV and link visibility, FDX revision marks, phone typing, a team merge. The round-2 "whatChangedIt" answers show nearly everyone deciding to wait for the influencer's review before acting. That makes the influencer's round-1 post the most persuasive one in the thread. It made the trial the agreed weak spot and turned "wait for a proper review" into the default.
- **The trial pile-on reinforced doubts but changed few answers.** Several personas said that seeing the same complaint from everyone made them more comfortable waiting. Bengaluru: "it's a pattern." The YouTube maker: "made me more comfortable waiting, not more eager to buy."
- **The veteran's caution spread; the corrections didn't land.** "Don't put your own story in" was picked up by the student, by the director ("made me more careful about loading my real script") and by the 1st AD. The director and Bengaluru both corrected the veteran: sync can be switched off and the exports exist. The veteran didn't move and recruited the line producer's budget point and Bengaluru's sign-in point as support.
- **The only clear peer-driven shift was the OTT writer moving the Celtx writer.** The OTT writer's point about missing revision colours and A-pages moved the Celtx writer to "more negative," and from maybe to no on paying.
- **The 1st AD and line producer formed a small alliance:** a parallel test with Excel alongside, call sheets on one side and budget on the other. The AD also talked the director down from buying in a rush: "Machi 31st deadline ku avasarapadaadha."

## 8. The price rise (question B)

- **It hardly moves anyone.** 6 of 10 said no effect. Their reasons were all the same: the after-rise price is still cheap, so there's nothing to hurry for. The line producer: "The countdown does not move me at Rs 999 versus 1,999." Bengaluru: "Even Rs 1,499 after the rise is cheap, so the deadline won't rush me." For the OTT writer, the company pays, so "the countdown means nothing."
- **Only one persona read it as a reason to buy now.** The indie director: "The founding price is real money saved and the deadline is honest". Even so, the director's purchase still depends on a friend confirming the tool works.
- **Three read it as pressure, and for a specific reason.** The problem is the deadline sitting next to a trial too short to judge from. The influencer: "with a 10-day price deadline next to it, the whole thing reads as pressure to buy before you can judge it. My followers will notice that combo." The 1st AD: "The 10-day deadline and the founding count feel like a sales push." The veteran: "The 30-minute trial and the countdown to the price rise feel like a sales trick to me." The Celtx writer, who answered "no effect," still wrote: "The 30-minute trial almost reads like they don't want me to look too closely."
- **The way the deadline is presented earns trust.** The real count (2 of 100), a real date and a minutes-only display stopped the most deadline-sensitive persona from calling it fake. The deadline isn't hurting; the short trial makes it look like a trap.
- **After the rise:** 6 said maybe, 4 said no. The four noes are the student and the YouTube maker (both said Rs 999 or the Pro tier is more than they need), plus the veteran and OTT writer, who wouldn't pay at any price. So the rise mainly loses the low-budget creators, and they weren't converting before it either.
- **Bottom line for B:** if the rise does anything, it's to pressure people, and only because the trial is so short. Nobody went to "yes" before 31 Oct in either round, and two personas dropped to "no."

## 9. What to change

1. **Make the trial long enough to run on the user's own script.** This is evidence from all 10 personas. The specific asks were "a day or two" (director), "a few days" (1st AD, Celtx writer), "a day or more" (Bengaluru) and "a week or so" for reviewers (influencer). The trial must allow importing your own Tanglish/FDX/PDF draft, not only the sample films.
2. **If the trial stays short, don't run the deadline next to it in launch posts.** Lead with the product and the chain, and put the founding price second. Consider letting anyone who starts a trial before 31 Oct keep the founding price for a fixed window afterwards. That separates "try it properly" from "buy before you can judge," which is the pairing three personas called pressure. Keep the real count and minutes display, because that is what earned the influencer's trust.
3. **Publish proof you can check without signing in.** That means a downloadable sample screenplay PDF (Tamil and English) to check margins and pagination, and a plain statement of what survives FDX import and export: dual dialogue, scene numbers, revision marks, Tamil rendering. Also add a short phone-screen video of Tanglish being typed and formatting itself. Evidence: Celtx writer, OTT writer, YouTube maker, influencer, director.
4. **Answer the budget-visibility worry at the point of doubt.** Until a hide-the-budget permission exists, explain how a call sheet reaches crew on WhatsApp, and whether that exposes the budget, next to the call-sheet feature. Mention that a screening-room pass shows only story, script and scenes. Nobody noticed that option, though it partly answers the worry. Evidence: line producer, veteran, and the 1st AD and influencer in round 2.
5. **Reword "local-first" and the privacy story.** Say "written to your browser first; sync can be switched off" on the start page, not only in the FAQ. State plainly what happens on day 15 offline and what the device check-in sends, and link the privacy policy section. Evidence: Bengaluru, veteran, influencer ("offline-tolerant, not local-first").
6. **Explain the tier placement in one line each.** Why own-key AI is Pro (the influencer suggested saying you pay for the pipeline, not the tokens), and why call sheets are Pro. Evidence: Bengaluru, 1st AD, YouTube maker.
7. **Run a reviewer and creator programme plus a film-school batch offer.** The thread showed one credible reviewer setting the agenda for nine people, and the student asked for a batch deal ("our whole batch will try it"). Give reviewers a longer trial with the test list the personas wrote: Tanglish import, a 30–40-day board with three scenes moved, the budget CSV, an FDX round trip, a two-person merge.
8. **Say who it isn't for yet.** State on the site that writers' rooms with live co-editing and coloured revision pages aren't supported yet, rather than leaving the OTT writer to infer it. Team billing with a GST invoice and confidentiality terms belong on the roadmap if production houses are a target. Evidence: OTT writer, Celtx writer.

## 10. Caveats

- These are LLM personas, not people. They were written to raise objections and tend to be articulate, reasonable and consistent. Real users are noisier, more impulsive and less likely to read a brief at all.
- All personas read the same full brief, including the FAQ and the honest gaps. Real visitors see a fraction of it, so objections that rest on fine print (budget link, device check-in) may be over-represented. Objections that come from things a brief can't show (speed, look, onboarding friction) are missing entirely.
- The near-unanimous trial complaint is partly a herding effect: in round 2 the personas read each other, and agreement compounds.
- The answer scale offered maybe as an easy middle option. Nobody chose "yes" on paying, which may say as much about the format as about intent.
- Ten personas is a tiny, hand-picked sample, and the counts above are not percentages of any market.
- Personas cannot use the product. Every judgement here is about the pitch, not the software.
