# FilmMakerStudio launch: simulated reaction study (21 Oct 2026)

## 1. What this is

This is a simulation. Ten AI personas, each written as a Tamil-film-industry archetype, read the public launch brief. In round 1 each one reacted on their own and gave private reasoning. In round 2 each read the others' posts and replied. No real person took part. The study is useful for finding objections, confusing wording and the questions people will ask in public. It is not a sales forecast, and the counts below describe ten simulated people, not a market.

## 2. Headline

- **(A) The idea lands and trust does not.** 7 of 10 were curious on first read and 9 of 10 would try it. Nobody would commit to paying before 1 Nov: there were 8 "maybe" and 2 "no" in round 1, and 7 "maybe" and 3 "no" after round 2. Almost everyone said price is not the barrier. The barriers are no reviews, an unstated trial length, final-sale purchases, and doubt about getting their work back out.
- **(A) The most damaging objection was one the brief created.** The brief lists FDX and Fountain only as imports and PDF and Word as exports. In round 2 the OTT writer pointed this out and two personas (Celtx writer, Bengaluru indie) turned more negative because of it. These were the only two shifts in the study.
- **(A) Round 2 moved almost nobody** (8 of 10 "no change"). The thread became a pile-on of agreement: everyone repeated the same unanswered questions, and the promised review from the tools influencer became the thing everyone said they were waiting for.
- **(B) The price rise mostly reads as pressure, not as a deadline.** 5 of 10 said it "feels like pressure, puts me off", 1 said it "makes me doubt the product", 3 said "no effect", and only 1 (the Chennai indie director) said it "pushes me to buy now". Several called it hype or a launch trick outright, because the trial length is unknown and purchases are final.
- **(B) The rise is not a reason for anyone to walk away later.** 8 of 10 said "maybe" to paying even after the rise. The rise did not create urgency, and it did not price anyone out except the film student.

## 5. Objections, ranked

1. **New, no reviews, no one has used it on a real shoot.** Raised by 10 of 10 personas.
   > "Nobody has used it on a real shoot yet." (line producer)
2. **Trial length not stated / no free plan.** Raised by 8 personas: student, AD, YouTube maker, Bengaluru, influencer, director, line producer, OTT writer.
   > "Trial evlo naal nu clear ah sollunga bro, free plan illana konjam kashtam." (YouTube maker)
3. **Exit and data safety: export formats, what survives a shutdown, browser storage.** Raised by 8 personas: director, AD, line producer, Celtx writer, OTT writer, Bengaluru, veteran, influencer.
   > "If they shut down, a PDF of 3 years of work is not a backup I can keep editing." (Celtx writer, round 2)
4. **Purchases are final.** Raised by 6 personas: student, AD, Celtx writer, Bengaluru, influencer, veteran (indirectly).
   > "No refunds + no reviews + small team." (Bengaluru indie)
5. **Countdown looks like fake urgency.** Raised by 7 personas: student, AD, line producer, veteran, Bengaluru, influencer, YouTube maker.
   > "Andha \"1 Nov-ku munnaadi vaangunga\" nu poster, saree kadai aadi sale maadhiri irukku." (veteran)
6. **Output fidelity for Tamil and Tanglish: PDF formatting, FDX round trip, dual dialogue, revision colours.** Raised by 5 personas: director, Celtx writer, OTT writer, student, influencer.
   > "does the PDF match standard format, so a production house won't send it back?" (Celtx writer)
7. **No Excel/CSV export for breakdown, schedule and DOOD.** Raised by 4 personas: AD, line producer, Bengaluru, director.
   > "Excel/xlsx export irukka?" (1st AD)
8. **Who can see what (role permissions, hiding vendor rates).** Raised by 3 personas: line producer, AD, director.
   > "who on my crew can see the budget vs just the call sheet?" (line producer)
9. **Source and date of the "dated Chennai rates".** Raised by 1 persona (line producer), echoed by the director in round 2.
   > "enna rates, yaaru check pannadhu, which date?" (line producer)
10. **Privacy and leaks; Google sign-in when the product claims to be local-first; the 14-day offline limit.** Raised by 2 personas (veteran, Bengaluru).
    > "If it's \"local-first\", why does it need Google sign-in, and why does offline stop after 14 days?" (Bengaluru indie)
11. **Phone writing with no native app.** Raised by 1 persona (YouTube maker).
12. **Multi-writer co-editing is limited.** Raised by 1 persona (OTT writer, who repeated it in round 2).

## 6. What people noticed

**Features that mattered:**
- **Tamil/Tanglish screenplay writer with auto-formatting.** Named by 8 personas, the strongest single pull. "Celtx free la Tamil type panna pain da." (student)
- **The single chain from script to breakdown, stripboard and call sheet.** Named by 6 personas: director, AD, line producer, YouTube maker, Bengaluru, influencer.
- **Call sheets sent on WhatsApp.** Named by 5 personas: director, AD, line producer, YouTube maker, influencer. The AD's whole case rests on it.
- **Rupee budget at Chennai rates.** Named by 3 personas (director, line producer, influencer). The line producer would only trust it with the sources shown.
- **Sample film preloaded.** At least 7 personas plan to open it first. It is the de facto trial.
- **Local-first, offline, own-key AI.** Named by the Bengaluru indie (own-key AI and local-first). The veteran said it was the only line that made him stop scrolling.
- **One payment, no auto-renew.** Credited only by the Bengaluru indie. The veteran still called it "a subscription to me."

**Mentioned by almost nobody:**
- The AI tools beyond the Bengaluru indie's own-key point. No one named coverage, dialogue pass or logline workshop.
- The Story steps and the eleven story structures and pacing view: zero mentions.
- Storyboard: zero mentions.
- Locked pages and numbered drafts: only the OTT writer and the Celtx writer, and only to say they are not the same as revision colours.
- Remove branding: zero.
- Drive backup was mentioned only as a worry ("will it restore?"), never as a selling point.

## 7. Word of mouth

- **The most persuasive post was the OTT writer's** X post noting "I only see FDX import listed". The Celtx writer and the Bengaluru indie both re-read the brief, confirmed it, and became the only two "more negative" shifts. The Celtx writer then repeated the point to the Bengaluru indie and the OTT writer ("So the script can come in and can't go back out"). The objection spread through the thread.
- **The Bengaluru indie's numbered list of four questions** became the shared checklist. Replies quoting "Q3" (what can I export if they shut down) came from the director, the line producer, the OTT writer, the YouTube maker, the veteran and the AD. The influencer adopted all four: "your 4 questions are exactly my review checklist."
- **The influencer became the panel's proxy for proof.** Seven of the other nine replied to the influencer: director, student, AD, line producer, Celtx writer, YouTube maker, OTT writer. Each asked the influencer to test their own concern (Tanglish on a phone, a day shift updating the call sheet, budget sources, the FDX comparison, co-editing). Several now say they will wait for that review, and it may not appear before 1 Nov.
- **There was a pile-on, but of agreement, not of anger.** Every "what changed it" said some version of "everyone is asking the same questions and nobody has answered." The veteran put it best: "It was other people's doubts lining up with mine." Agreement made the doubts feel confirmed, not resolved.
- **Nobody moved anybody positively.** The only practical advice in the thread came from the Bengaluru indie to the director: "Turn on the Drive backup on day one and don't treat it as optional." The influencer told the student: "Try first, decide later."

## 8. The price rise

- **It reads as pressure, not as a deadline.** 6 of 10 reacted negatively: 5 said it feels like pressure and puts them off, and 1 said it makes them doubt the product. Only the indie director said the rise "pushes me to buy now", and even he said "I won't pay until I've tested an import of my own script."
- **Why it reads that way:** three facts in the brief combine.
  1. Ten days notice on launch day for a product with no reviews.
  2. A trial that "may be offered" with its length "set by the studio".
  3. Final-sale purchases.

  Together they make the deadline look like a funnel. The influencer named the risk outright: "My followers will call it a fake-urgency funnel." The AD said it is "set by their sales calendar, not my shoot calendar." The veteran compared it to an aadi sale.
- **The amount itself barely matters.** Nearly every persona called Rs 599–999 trivial. The AD would pay "even at Rs 1,999", and the line producer called it "less than one day's tea and snacks on set." The rise does not change the value they see. It changes how trustworthy the seller looks.
- **Who it actually affects on price:** only the film student, the one persona who would not pay after the rise ("still a few weeks of canteen money").
- **Unstated trial plus deadline is the worst combination.** The YouTube maker: "2 naal dhaan na oru episode kooda ezhudha mudiyaadhu." Without a stated trial length, nobody can tell whether they have time to test before 1 Nov, so the deadline only pressures them.

## 9. What to change

1. **State the trial length in a number on the start page and in every launch post.** 8 of 10 asked for it. Several set a floor of about a week ("at least 7 days", "a week or two", "one real prep week"). If the trial runs past 1 Nov for anyone who starts it before 1 Nov, say so. That one sentence turns the rise from pressure into a fair deadline.
2. **List every export the product has, especially FDX and Fountain.** This was the round's most damaging objection and it came from the brief's wording. (Analyst note, from the repository, not the simulation: `src/pages/screenplay.js` already offers "Export Final Draft (.fdx)" and "Export Fountain (.fountain)", and breakdown, budget costs and the shot list have CSV exports. If those ship, the brief undersold them.) Add a plain "Your work, out in open formats" line: FDX, Fountain, PDF, Word, CSV, and the Drive backup file.
3. **Answer the Bengaluru indie's four questions on the page, in his words.** Why Google sign-in, why 14 days offline, the trial length, and what leaves the browser and when. These became the thread's shared checklist. The influencer said a straight answer goes in the video and a dodge goes in the video too.
4. **Reframe the rise as founding pricing for early users rather than a countdown.** For example: "Founding price for anyone who starts before 1 Nov", with no ticking timer and the trial stated alongside it. Pair it with a reason, such as "you are using a new product; you pay less for that." Six personas read the current framing as a trick.
5. **Show proof the chain works, in a short screen recording.** The AD asked for one: move a day on a Tamil feature's stripboard, show the call sheet and DOOD updating, then show the PDF on WhatsApp. Also show a close-up of Tanglish dialogue in the exported PDF next to a Final Draft one (director, Celtx writer, influencer). The sample film already contains this; record it.
6. **On the budget, show the source and the check date per rate line, and say who can see the budget.** The line producer's trust depends on both. If role-based visibility does not exist, say so plainly rather than leaving it unanswered. Three personas asked.
7. **Give one named, reachable person behind the product, and a press/review account.** Requested by the veteran ("A real name and face behind the company") and the influencer, who wants a review account before 1 Nov. This must stay consistent with the owner's decision not to display the owner's name: a named team contact or founder handle works.
8. **Be explicit about first-purchase cases and offline limits.** Restate the refund exceptions as a short, friendly list near the buy button. Explain the 14-day offline grace as a feature ("works on set without signal for two weeks"), not a footnote. Four personas cited purchases being final as a reason to wait.

## 10. Caveats

- Ten LLM personas are not ten people. They share one model's priors, which makes the strong consensus (everyone worried about trust and the trial) partly an artefact.
- The personas saw only the brief, not the product. Every objection about capabilities reflects what the brief says, which is why the FDX finding is a wording problem first.
- Round-2 behaviour is a single exchange. Real threads include people who have actually tried the product, which this one structurally could not have. That is exactly why "nobody has tested it" dominated.
- The structured answers ("maybe") hide variation in how close each persona is to buying. Read the reasoning, not just the tallies.
- The panel is skewed towards working professionals and has no producer or company buyer. Price sensitivity is probably understated for students and hobbyists.
- None of this predicts conversion, revenue or the effect of the 1 Nov rise on real sales.
