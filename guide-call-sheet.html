How to make a film call sheet

The call sheet is the one page every person on the unit reads before a shoot day. Here is what it must say, in what order, and how to get it to people who read the production group on their phone.

Updated 26 October 2026

On this page

  1. What a call sheet is for
  2. What it must say
  3. Step by step
  4. The WhatsApp version
  5. In the studio

What a call sheet is for

A call sheet tells each person where to be, when, and what will be shot. It is made from the schedule the evening before, once the day is settled, and it replaces every earlier message about that day.

If two versions of a call sheet are going round, the unit will follow the wrong one. Send one sheet, and if anything changes, send the whole sheet again with the change marked.

What it must say

  • The production, the shoot day number and the date.
  • The general call: the time the unit is expected, and the expected wrap.
  • Each location with a full address and a map link, plus where to park and where the unit base is.
  • The scenes in shooting order: scene number, slug line, day or night, page length and the cast in each.
  • Each person's own call time, which is often earlier than the general call for make-up, art and camera.
  • Sunrise and sunset, so exterior scenes are planned against the light.
  • The nearest hospital and police station to each location.
  • Who to call: the first assistant director and the production manager, with phone numbers.
  • Notes for the day: special equipment, a stunt, an animal, a child artist, or a permission to carry.

Step by step

  1. Lock the day on the schedule. Decide which scenes are being shot and in what order. The call sheet copies the schedule; it is not where the schedule is decided.
  2. List who is needed. Read the cast from the scenes, then add the crew the day needs. Anyone not on the sheet is not called.
  3. Set each call time. Work backwards from the first shot: make-up and costume before cast, camera and lights before the first set-up.
  4. Add the location facts. Address, map link, parking, unit base, and the nearest hospital and police station.
  5. Check and send the night before. Read it once against the schedule, then send it to everyone at the same time, early enough that people can plan their travel.

The WhatsApp version

Many units read the production group on a phone rather than open a PDF. A plain-text message works better there: the person's own call time on the first line, then the location, the scenes and the contacts. Keep it short enough to read on a lock screen and send the full sheet as well for the heads of department.

In the studio

Call sheets are built from your scenes and your cast and crew list, one sheet per shoot day. Tick the scenes being shot and their locations and page lengths come from the script; pick who is called and give each person a time.

Sunrise, sunset and golden hour are worked out on your device. The nearest hospital and police station come from the location recce if you filled them in.

Print the sheet, or send it as a WhatsApp message to the whole unit or to one person with their own call time first. A person's details are stored once, so a corrected phone number fixes every sheet they are on.
