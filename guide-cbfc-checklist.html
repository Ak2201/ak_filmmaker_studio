A CBFC certification checklist

What to prepare before you apply for a CBFC certificate, read from the sources listed at the foot of this page. It is a checklist, not legal advice: check anything that matters with your regional CBFC office or a lawyer.

Updated 26 October 2026

On this page

  1. The categories
  2. If tobacco is on screen
  3. If drugs are shown
  4. If an animal is in the film
  5. If a child under 14 works on the film
  6. What the Board weighs
  7. In the studio

The categories

Since the Cinematograph (Amendment) Act, 2023 and the Cinematograph (Certification) Rules, 2024, a film is certified U, UA 7+, UA 13+, UA 16+ or A. The three UA markers mean unrestricted exhibition with parental guidance for children under that age. The 2024 Rules also moved applications to the e-Cinepramaan portal.

The Board judges the finished film as a whole, so a category can only be planned for, never guaranteed.

If tobacco is on screen

  • Anti-tobacco health spots of at least 30 seconds at the beginning and the middle of the film.
  • An audio-visual disclaimer on the ill effects of tobacco, at least 20 seconds, at the beginning and the middle.
  • A static health warning at the bottom of the screen while tobacco is shown, in the language of the film.
  • An editorial justification for the display, given to the Board with the application.
  • No brand: packs and logos are masked or replaced by the art department.

If drugs are shown

The certification guidelines revised in September 2026 ask that scenes showing narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances carry the prescribed on-screen message, and that the film does not encourage, justify or glamorise drug use.

If an animal is in the film

The Animal Welfare Board of India gives its no-objection only after a pre-shoot permission, applied for online before the shoot with the owner's details and a fitness certificate. Some animals, including bulls, may not be exhibited or trained as performing animals at all, so check that before the scene is written into the schedule.

If a child under 14 works on the film

Take the District Magistrate's permission for the district where you are shooting, for every child artist, before the work starts. The rules also limit the working day and require rest, supervision and arrangements for schooling; read them before you schedule.

What the Board weighs

  • Violence, and how long the camera stays on it.
  • Sexual content and sexual violence, kept to the minimum the story needs.
  • Abusive language: record a clean alternative line for any word you may have to lose, and remember subtitles count.
  • Contempt for a community, and the use of national emblems, the flag and uniforms.
  • Real people and brands: a defamation and trade-mark question as well as a certification one.

In the studio

The Deliverables page has a certification tab that searches your script for content the certification process asks about: tobacco, drugs, alcohol, animals, violence, language, children and more, in English, Tanglish and a starter list of Tamil-script terms.

Each flag shows the scenes it came from, what the rule asks for and the source it rests on. It is a word search, not a reading of the film, and the page says so; the likely-category line is the studio's own guess, not the Board's criteria.
