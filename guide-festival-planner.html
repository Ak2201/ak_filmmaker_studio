Planning a film festival run

A festival run is a sequence, not a list. The order you send the film out in decides which festivals can still take it, so plan it before you submit anywhere.

Updated 26 October 2026

On this page

  1. The premiere decides the order
  2. Choose your tiers
  3. Build the calendar backwards
  4. Have these ready
  5. In the studio

The premiere decides the order

Many major festivals only select films that have not screened anywhere else first: a world, international or national premiere. Posting the film online counts as a screening. So the usual advice is to hold any online release until the festivals that want a premiere have answered.

Read each festival's premiere rule before you submit, and keep a note of which festivals are still holding a claim on your film.

Choose your tiers

  • A few top-tier festivals with strict premiere rules, sent to first.
  • A wider set of festivals that accept films that have screened before.
  • Festivals close to your audience: regional, language and genre festivals in India.
  • Online showcases last, once the premiere-holding festivals are done.

Build the calendar backwards

Write down each festival's deadline from its own website, for this year's edition, and work back: the date the screener must be ready, the date the subtitles must be final, the date the entry fee must be paid. Deadlines and rules change every year, so never plan from last year's dates or an aggregator listing.

Many festivals have early and late deadlines with different fees. Short and feature deadlines can differ too, which is an easy and expensive thing to misread.

Have these ready

  • A private screener link, and the film as an H.264 file.
  • English subtitles, and a version with them burned in if the festival asks.
  • A DCP or ProRes file for festivals that screen from one.
  • Stills, a poster, a logline and synopses of different lengths, and a director's statement and photograph.
  • A budget line for entry fees, and a record of what you sent where and when.

In the studio

The Short Film guide's festival step holds a catalogue of eighteen festivals by tier, each with its premiere rule, and some with this edition's dates read from the festival's own site on the day shown on its card. A festival that was not re-checked says so instead of showing a guessed date.
