An OTT delivery checklist

When a platform licenses your film, it sends a list of what to deliver. This checklist helps you plan for that list before the grade and the mix are locked, when changes are still cheap.

Updated 26 October 2026

On this page

  1. Ask for the spec first
  2. Picture
  3. Sound
  4. Text and paperwork
  5. What the platforms publish
  6. In the studio

Ask for the spec first

Every platform's own delivery specification, and the list it issues for your title, wins over any general checklist, this one included. Ask for it with the deal memo, before the grade and mix are locked.

Picture

  • A graded master in a high-quality format, such as ProRes 4444 or 422 HQ.
  • A textless or non-subtitled master: the picture with no burned-in subtitles, so subtitles and dubs can be added as separate files.
  • An HDR master only if the platform asks for one; a licensed SDR film may not need it.
  • A trailer or clips, if the platform asks.

Sound

  • A 5.1 printmaster and a stereo mix.
  • Music and effects (M&E) stems, and dialogue stems if asked, for dubbing. Make them while you mix; recovering them later is expensive.
  • A music cue sheet listing every piece of music and its rights.

Text and paperwork

  • Subtitles and captions in the format the platform names, timed to the master.
  • A dialogue list, which is the subtitler's starting point.
  • The CBFC certificate details, which many Indian platforms show with the title.
  • Chain of title and releases for cast, crew, locations, music and artwork.
  • Errors and omissions insurance, if the contract requires it.
  • Key art, stills, synopses and the full credit list.

What the platforms publish

Netflix publishes its delivery specifications: picture and sound go as an IMF package (SMPTE ST 2067-21, Application #2E), timed text as TTML1, and each title gets its own Delivery Requirements List, which wins over the general specification.

Prime Video publishes Tech Docs: a constant-frame-rate video mezzanine, a semi-textless master (no burned-in subtitles in any language), one audio asset per language, and a list of accepted timed-text formats.

For Aha, Sun NXT and ZEE5 we could not find a public delivery specification when we checked on 7 October 2026. Ask their acquisitions team for it in writing, and plan from that.

In the studio

The Deliverables page is a checklist of what leaves with the film: picture masters, sound, subtitles, certification and chain of title, and publicity materials, with optional groups for Netflix, Prime Video, and templates for Aha, Sun NXT and ZEE5 to fill in from their spec.

Each platform group names the source it was read from and when. The dialogue list for your subtitler is built from your screenplay.
