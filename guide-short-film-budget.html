How to budget a short film in rupees

A budget is a list of decisions with a number beside each. Here is how to build one for a short film in India, and how to keep it honest once money starts moving.

Updated 26 October 2026

On this page

  1. Start from the schedule, not a total
  2. The lines a short film needs
  3. Rates: write down where each number came from
  4. Once the shoot starts
  5. In the studio

Start from the schedule, not a total

Most of a short film's cost grows with the number of shoot days: crew fees, bata, equipment hire, food, transport and locations are all paid per day. So schedule first, count the days, and let the days set those lines.

A figure decided before the schedule, then forced onto it, is how a two-day shoot quietly becomes three days on a two-day budget.

The lines a short film needs

  • Cast: fees, and bata for each day an actor works.
  • Crew: fees by department, and bata per working day.
  • Camera, lenses, lights and sound: hire per day, plus any extra day for a test or a pick-up.
  • Locations: hire, permissions and any police or corporation fees.
  • Art and costume: what is bought, what is hired, and what has to be made.
  • Food and transport for the whole unit, per day.
  • Post-production: edit, sound mix, grade, subtitles and music.
  • Festival and distribution: entry fees, the files each festival asks for, and posters.
  • Contingency: a share of the total kept back for what goes wrong.

Rates: write down where each number came from

A rate you heard last year is not a quote. Beside each figure, note who quoted it and when, so you can tell an old number from a current one. Where a rate is negotiated, the agreed figure is the one that goes in the budget, not a band.

Bata (the daily allowance for crew on a shoot day) is easy to forget because it is small per person. Across a unit and several days it is often one of the larger lines.

Once the shoot starts

Log every expense against the line it belongs to, and keep petty cash in its own record with a receipt or a note for each payment. Check spent against planned at the end of each day, not at wrap: an overrun found on day two can still be fixed.

Before the shoot, lock a version of the budget. When something changes, you can compare the new figure with the version everyone agreed.

In the studio

The estimator starts from Chennai rate bands and a FEFSI bata table, each marked with when it was last checked and never inflated to look current. You can edit any figure to what you agreed.

Lines that grow with shoot days can take their days from your stripboard; only blank fields are filled, so your own entries stay yours.

During the shoot, log expenses and petty cash against the plan, set a contingency, and lock budget versions to compare. The budget, actuals and expenses download as CSV.
