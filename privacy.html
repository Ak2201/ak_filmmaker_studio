Privacy Policy

What we collect, why we collect it, who helps us process it, and the rights you have over it.

Last updated 21 October 2026

Your work is saved on your device first. Everything you write in {{brand:name}} — scripts, breakdowns, schedules, budgets, contacts and notes — is saved in your own browser. It is copied to our database only while you are signed in as a member with cloud sync on (it is on by default, and you can turn it off in Settings), and to your Google Drive only if Drive backup is connected.

We never sell your personal data, we do not show advertising, we run no third-party analytics, and we do not use your scripts or projects to train AI models.

What changed on 21 October 2026

  • New section 9, for people in the United States: the California (CCPA/CPRA) categories of personal information we collect, that we do not sell or share it, how to ask for access or deletion, and that exercising a right never changes your price or your service.
  • Section 2: how we choose whether to show you prices in rupees or US dollars (in your browser, with no IP lookup), and that a payment record now notes its currency and whether the card was issued outside India.
  • Section 4: using the studio from outside India transfers your data to India and to the countries our providers work in.
  • On 19 October 2026: cloud sync, payment records, receipt e-mails, invoices, session records and sign-in history, team invitations, screening passes, the Chrome extension, support records, account deletion and who can see your data were described as they work; Google Fonts and the launch-offers form were removed.

1. Who we are

{{brand:name}} (“we”, “us”) is an online set of tools for filmmakers, provided from India by a sole proprietor; {{brand:name}} is a trading name, not a registered company. For the purposes of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (“DPDP Act”), we are the Data Fiduciary for the personal data described in this policy. You can reach us at {{brand:email}}.

2. What we collect

Before you sign in

Our public pages (the start page, pricing, the product pages and these policies) can be read without an account. The studio itself opens after you sign in with Google. If you enter an invite code before signing in, the code is kept in your browser until it has been checked against the account you sign in with, and a short-lived ticket (ten minutes) is kept in the tab’s session storage so the code survives the trip to Google and back.

When you sign in with Google

  • Account details — your name, e-mail address and profile picture as provided by Google, Google’s identifier for your account, and an internal account identifier. We never see your Google password.
  • Access records — whether you are a member and how you became one (an invite code, an approved request, a free trial, a purchase, or access we gave you by e-mail), when a free trial started and how long it runs, and which invite code you redeemed.
  • Invite requests — if you ask for access, your e-mail address and name as Google gives them, the optional note you write, the type of browser you sent it from, and whether it was approved or declined.
  • Session records — your account is used on one device at a time. To do that we keep one record per account: a random session identifier, when the device last checked in (about every 30 seconds while the studio is open), and that device’s IP address and browser type. The IP address is visible to our administrators; if another device holds your session, you are shown only its browser type and when it last checked in. The IP address is never used to grant access.
  • Sign-in history — separately, our sign-in provider (Supabase) keeps a record of each sign-in: when it started, the IP address and the browser type. Our administrators can see your five most recent. These records end when you sign out of that sign-in, and are deleted with your account.

Synced project data

While you are signed in as a member and cloud sync is on, each project you work on is copied to our database a few seconds after you stop typing, so that it is the same on every device you sign in on. That covers what you write in the studio: the blueprints and their settings, story and characters, the screenplay and its drafts, scenes, shots, locations and the schedule, contacts (including any phone numbers and e-mail addresses you type for your crew), costs, songs, festival plans, the idea vault, the edit log, deliverables, writing goals and the activity log. The scene bin stays on your device only. Comments, share links and collaborator lists you create are stored with the project. If you invite someone to a seat on your team (organisation) account, we store the e-mail address you invite, the role you give them and whether they have joined.

You can turn cloud sync off at any time in Settings → Cloud sync; changes then stay on that device until you turn it back on. Settings → Danger zone → “Delete my cloud projects” deletes the cloud copy of every project you own and leaves the copies on your device.

If you enter other people’s details — a crew member’s phone number, say — you are responsible for having their permission to keep them.

Which currency we show you

Our pages show prices in Indian Rupees to visitors in India and in US dollars to everyone else. Your browser works out which from your device’s time zone and, if that says nothing, its language settings. This happens on your device: we make no location or IP lookup for it, and the guess is not sent to us. If you pick a currency yourself on the purchase page, the choice is kept in your browser’s storage on that device (and in backup files you download), not on our servers.

When you buy a plan

Payments are processed by Razorpay. Your full card number, CVV, UPI PIN and banking passwords are entered with Razorpay and never reach us. When you start a purchase we give Razorpay your name and e-mail address, to fill in its checkout, and our internal identifier for your account, to match the payment to it. We keep:

  • the order: plan, amount, currency (Indian Rupees or US dollars), date, status (for example paid or refunded), the Razorpay order and payment identifiers, and any promo or referral code used;
  • the payment record Razorpay sends us when a payment is confirmed. Depending on how you paid, it can include the payment method, the e-mail address and phone number you gave at checkout, your UPI ID, the name of your bank or wallet, or, for a card, its network, type, issuer and last four digits. For any payment, we also record whether Razorpay reports the card as issued outside India and, when it says, the country that issued it;
  • the invoice we issue for the purchase (a Bill of Supply): its number, your name and e-mail address, a GSTIN if you give us one, the plan, the amount and the date;
  • refund records and any refund request you send (its reason category and your message); and
  • if you have a referral code, the credits it has earned and whether they have been paid out.

Receipts and refund e-mails

After a purchase we e-mail a receipt to your account’s address, and we e-mail you again when a refund is processed or fails. These are sent through Resend, which receives your name and e-mail address to deliver them. They carry the invoice number, the plan, the amount, the date and the Razorpay payment or refund identifier. We do not send marketing e-mail without your consent.

When you use AI features

AI tools work only with an API key that you supply for Anthropic or Google Gemini. The key is stored in your browser on your device, is never included in backups or cloud sync, and is never sent to us. When you run an AI tool, the text it shows you it will send goes directly from your browser to that provider and is handled under the provider’s own terms and privacy policy. Storyboard pictures drawn with Gemini are saved, when you accept them, as files in your own Google Drive; the project keeps only a reference to each file.

When you write to us

Support works by e-mail. When a message needs following up, an administrator may log it as a ticket with your e-mail address, a subject and a summary, and may keep short internal notes on your account (for example, “asked about an invoice”).

If you open a screening pass

A screening pass opens a read-only copy of one project for a guest without an account. When you open one you may be asked for your e-mail address; it is not checked. Each opening is logged with that address and a short access code, and both are printed as a watermark across the page. Our administrators can see the log.

Technical data

Like any website, our hosting provider receives your IP address and basic request information (such as browser type and the page requested) when you load a page, and our database provider does the same when the studio talks to it. Both may keep short-lived logs for security and reliability. We do not run third-party analytics, advertising trackers or session recording. The one thing we count ourselves is described next.

Counts of which tools are used (signed in)

While you are signed in, {{brand:name}} adds one to a daily total each time you open one of its tools (for example the budget or the stripboard) or use one of eight features: importing a script, exporting a PDF, exporting a Word file, an AI request, connecting Google Drive, creating a share link, using a screening pass, and meeting a plan limit. A total is a row of three things — the day, the name of the tool or feature, and your plan — plus a number. It has no name, e-mail, account identifier, film title, script text, note, file, prompt or AI answer on it, and the names it can hold are a fixed list, so nothing you write can reach it. Because a total does not say whose it is, it cannot be traced back to you or taken back out.

What is kept about you for a moment: so that nobody can flood the totals, we keep an hourly call count against your account identifier for 48 hours and then delete it. We use that count only to say how many people used the studio in the last day or two. We cannot say how many used it in a week, by design. The same fixed-list, counts-only rule applies to a second set of totals that records when a save to your account was refused and the plain reason code (for example “plan limit”), with the name of the kind of data and never its content.

Why: to see which tools are used and which are not, and to notice when saving is failing for someone. Retention: the daily totals are kept for as long as {{brand:name}} runs, because they identify nobody.

How to turn it off: Settings → Privacy → “Count which tools I use (no content, ever)”. The choice is kept on your device and takes effect from your next click; it also stops the refused-save reports. When you are not signed in, nothing is counted. We send the totals once, as you leave or hide the page; we never send them in the background.

Visit counts and the old launch-offers list

We count visits to the start page and the pricing section, invite requests, sign-ups, checkouts started and purchases as plain daily totals, with no identifier, IP address or cookie attached, so a count cannot be traced to you. We honour Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control, and the start page has a “Don’t count this visit” button.

Our start page used to have a “Get launch offers” form. It has been removed. If you used it, we still hold the address, the exact consent sentence you saw, and the date; we use it only to write to you about {{brand:name}}’s launch and offers, only an administrator can read the list, and it is not shared. Ask us at {{brand:email}} and we will delete it.

3. Why we use it

  • To provide the service you asked for: signing you in, syncing and sharing your projects, and backing them up.
  • To run access to the studio: free trials, invite codes, invite requests and memberships, and keeping each account to one device at a time.
  • To activate the plan you bought, issue invoices and receipts, keep payment records required by law, and handle refunds and referral credits.
  • To answer your support requests.
  • To keep the service secure, prevent abuse, and enforce our Terms of Service.
  • To contact you about your account, a purchase or an important change to the service. We do not send marketing e-mail without your consent.

We process your personal data on the basis of the consent you give when you sign in, sync, connect a service or make a purchase, and for the legitimate uses the DPDP Act permits, such as complying with law.

4. Who processes it for us

We use a small number of trusted service providers (Data Processors), each only for the purpose listed:

  • Supabase — sign-in, the database that holds synced projects, comments, sharing, account, membership and payment records, and the server functions that talk to Razorpay and Resend.
  • Google — Google sign-in, and Google Drive for the backup file and storyboard pictures. Google’s sign-in script is loaded from accounts.google.com when Drive needs it, and your profile picture is shown from Google’s servers.
  • Razorpay — payment processing and refunds. Its checkout is loaded from Razorpay when you start a purchase.
  • Resend — delivering receipt and refund e-mails.
  • YouTube (Google) — only if you press play on one of the explainer videos. Nothing is loaded from YouTube before that: the player then loads from youtube-nocookie.com, which receives your IP address and may store data in your browser under Google’s privacy policy.
  • Vercel and/or Netlify — website hosting and delivery.
  • Anthropic or Google (Gemini) — only when you run an AI tool with your own key; you have the direct relationship with that provider.

Our typefaces are served from our own site, so loading a page does not contact a font service. Some of these providers store or process data outside India. Where they do, they process it on our instructions and under their own security commitments. We run the service from India, so if you use it from another country, your personal data is transferred to India and to the countries where these providers operate. We share personal data with authorities only when the law requires us to.

5. Google Drive backup

When you sign in, Google’s screen also asks for the drive.file permission, which lets the studio see only the files it created. It cannot read anything else in your Drive. If you allow it and your plan includes Drive backup, the studio connects straight away and writes a single backup file of your studio, which it keeps up to date (or, while cloud sync is on, updates when you back up). You can also connect or reconnect from Settings. Accepted storyboard pictures are saved as files in your Drive too. You can open, download or delete these files at any time.

Disconnecting Drive never deletes the backup file. “Erase everything on this device” in Settings deletes it only if you tick the box that offers to. You can remove the studio’s access from your Google account permissions page.

6. Cookies and browser storage

We do not use advertising or tracking cookies. The studio uses your browser’s local storage to save your work and preferences on your device, and session storage for things that should last only until you close the tab. Our sign-in provider stores a session in your browser so that you stay signed in. If you play an explainer video, YouTube’s player may store its own data in your browser; it is not loaded until you press play. Clearing your browser’s site data removes all of this from that device — including any work you have not backed up.

In the Chrome extension

The {{brand:name}} extension opens the studio in Chrome’s side panel. Its sign-in lives in Chrome’s session storage, which is cleared when the browser closes, and it checks in with our database about every 30 seconds to keep your account on one device at a time. If you select text on a web page and choose “Send to {{brand:name}}”, the selected text, the page’s address and its title are kept in the extension’s storage until the studio adds them to your Idea Vault. The extension reads nothing else from the pages you visit. Its background part talks only to our database; the studio pages it opens in the side panel work exactly as they do on the website.

7. How long we keep it

  • Account and synced project data — for as long as your account is active. You can delete your cloud projects yourself at any time (see section 2). When you ask us to delete your account, we do it within 30 days: your sign-in, synced projects, payment records, team, support tickets, access grants and any launch-offers entry are deleted.
  • What deletion keeps — invoices, because the law requires them, with your name, e-mail address and GSTIN removed; a one-way fingerprint (hash) of your e-mail address, so that it is not added back to our lists; the log of actions our administrators took, which records what was done and when, never your project content, and which can include your e-mail address where an action was about it (access given to an address, for example) or where you were an administrator yourself; and comments you left on other people’s projects, which stay with your name replaced by “Deleted user”.
  • Invoices — for as long as Indian tax and accounting law requires (generally up to eight years).
  • Invite requests — until they are decided and for a reasonable period afterwards, so that repeat requests can be handled fairly.
  • Session records — one per account, replaced each time you start a session on a new device.
  • Sign-in history — kept by our sign-in provider until you sign out of that sign-in, and deleted with your account.
  • Per-account call counts behind the usage totals — 48 hours.
  • Server logs — for a short period set by our hosting and database providers.
  • Data on your device — until you delete it; it is under your control, not ours. Settings → Danger zone → “Erase everything on this device” removes it in one step.

8. Your rights

Under the DPDP Act you have the right to:

  • get a summary of the personal data we hold about you and how we use it;
  • have inaccurate or incomplete data corrected and updated;
  • have your data erased when it is no longer needed, or withdraw your consent (withdrawal does not affect anything done before it, and may mean we can no longer provide cloud features);
  • have your grievances addressed by us; and
  • nominate another person to exercise these rights on your behalf if you die or become incapacitated.

To use any of these rights, e-mail {{brand:email}} from the address on your account. We will respond within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with our response, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India. If you live in the United States, section 9 sets out the rights you also have there.

9. If you live in the United States (including California)

This section is for residents of California under the California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (“CCPA”), and for residents of other US states with similar privacy laws. We give the rights below to anyone in the United States who asks, whether or not a particular state’s law applies to a service of our size. Sections 2 to 7 and 10 describe our practices in full; this section maps them to the CCPA’s terms.

Categories of personal information we collect

In the last 12 months we have collected these categories of personal information, each described in section 2:

  • Identifiers — your name, e-mail address and profile picture from Google, Google’s identifier for your account, our internal account identifier, your session identifier and your IP address.
  • Customer records and commercial information — the plans you bought, their amounts and currency, invoices, refunds and refund requests, promo and referral codes and credits, and the payment record Razorpay sends us (the payment method, the phone number and e-mail address given at checkout, a UPI ID, a bank or wallet name, or a card’s network, type, issuer and last four digits, and whether and where it was issued outside India). We never receive a full card number, CVV, UPI PIN or banking password.
  • Internet or other electronic network activity — session check-ins and sign-in history (time, IP address and browser type), invite requests, and screening-pass openings.
  • Content you give us — projects you sync, comments, share links, team invitations, support messages, and the details of other people you choose to enter (a crew member’s phone number, for example).

We do not collect precise geolocation, government identification numbers or account passwords, and we do not use personal information to infer characteristics about you. The daily usage totals in section 2 carry no identifier and are not personal information. We get personal information from you, from Google when you sign in, from Razorpay when you pay, and from your browser when it talks to our servers. We use it for the purposes in section 3, keep it for the periods in section 7, and disclose it, for those purposes only, to the service providers in section 4 and to the people you choose to share a project with.

No sale and no sharing

We do not sell personal information and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as the CCPA defines “sell” and “share”, and we have not done either in the last 12 months. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18 (section 11). Because we neither sell nor share, there is nothing to opt out of; we use personal information only to provide the service, so there is no use of sensitive personal information to limit either. We also honour Global Privacy Control signals (section 2).

Your rights

  • To know and access the categories and the specific pieces of personal information we hold about you, where it came from, why we use it and who we disclose it to.
  • To delete personal information we hold about you, subject to what the law lets or requires us to keep (section 7: invoices, for example, with your name and e-mail address removed).
  • To correct inaccurate personal information.
  • Not to be discriminated against for using any of these rights: we will not refuse you the service, charge you a different price, give you a different level or quality of service, or suggest that we will, because you made a request. Prices differ between rupees and US dollars for everyone, never because of a request.

How to make a request

E-mail {{brand:email}} from the address on your account and say which right you are using. We verify a request by matching it to your account’s e-mail address, and may ask you to confirm it from that address. Someone you authorise may make a request for you with your signed permission; we may still ask you to confirm it. We acknowledge a request within 10 business days and answer it within 45 days; if we need longer, we tell you why and take no more than 45 days more. If we decline a request, you may write back and ask us to reconsider; we answer that within 45 days too. There is no charge for a request.

10. Security, and who can see your data

Data in transit is encrypted with HTTPS. Our database checks every request against the signed-in account, so synced data can be reached only by you and the people listed below.

  • Collaborators — a share link lets someone who signs in see, comment on or edit the whole project, as you chose when you made the link, until it expires or you remove it.
  • Team owners and admins — if a project belongs to a team (organisation) account, that team’s owner and admins can open it.
  • Screening passes — a pass is created from our administrator console for one project, and we create one only when that project’s owner asks us to. Anyone holding it can read that project’s story, blueprint and scenes — never its contacts or budget — until it expires or runs out of openings.
  • Our administrators — can see your account details, how you got access, your plan, payments, invoices, refund requests, invite requests, session record, sign-in history, and how many projects you sync and how large they are. The console does not show your project content. It does show the title of a project that has a share link (with its owner’s e-mail address) or a screening pass. Every change an administrator makes to an account is recorded in a log that cannot be edited from the console. The data export we use to answer a request for your data contains your synced projects, content included; only the studio’s owner can make one. As the operator of the database we can technically reach synced content; we do not open it unless you ask us to help with it, you ask for your data, or the law requires it.

No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affecting your personal data occurs, we will notify you and the Data Protection Board as the law requires.

11. Children

{{brand:name}} is meant for adults. You must be at least 18 years old to create an account or make a purchase. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children; if you believe a child has given us personal data, contact us and we will delete it.

12. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. The date at the top shows the latest version, and the note under it says what changed. If a change is significant, we will let signed-in members know in the app or by e-mail before it takes effect.
