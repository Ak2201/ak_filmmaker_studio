Everything it does

Every tool a film needs, from the idea to the delivery

Five stages, one film. Each tool reads the same scene list, so a change in the script shows up in the schedule, the call sheet and the budget without being typed again.

1 · Story

Find the film

  • Guided story path Logline, characters, conflict, stakes and theme, one question at a time, with a worked example.
  • Beats & Pacing Write the story and check its shape: where it rises, where it sags.
  • Idea Vault Links, pictures and notes for this film, kept in one place.
  • Pitch Deck A short PDF that sells your film to investors, made in one click.

2 · Screenplay

Write it properly

  • Screenplay Writer Slug lines, action, dialogue and transitions format themselves; the page count is honest. Tamil, Tanglish or English.
  • Drafts Numbered drafts: compare, restore, print.
  • Title Page Title, credit, author, draft, date and contact on page 1 of every PDF.
  • Import Screenplay Bring in a Fountain, Final Draft, PDF or text screenplay, and export a proper screenplay PDF.

3 · Pre-Production, the script

Understand what you wrote

  • Screenplay Analysis Pages, runtime, scenes, cast, locations, outline and characters.
  • Scene List Every scene in order, where it happens, day or night.
  • Screen Time Minutes each scene will run on screen, worked out from the pages.
  • Cast Matrix Which character is in which scene, and who shares the most.
  • Auto-Tag Reads the script and suggests what each scene calls for: cast, background, props, set dressing, wardrobe, hair and makeup, vehicles, stunts, animals, sound, special effects, visual effects and special equipment. You accept what is right.
  • Scene Breakdown & Elements Mark what each scene needs, from set dressing to SFX, then see everything the film needs, listed once with its scenes. Recount each scene’s length from the script when it changes.

3 · Pre-Production, the look

See it before you shoot it

  • Shot List The shots for each scene: how close the camera is and how it moves.
  • Storyboard A drawing or picture for each shot you plan.
  • Blocking An overhead map for each setup: drag the actors, the camera and their marks into place.
  • Scene Board One scene on one printable page: its script, shots, storyboard frames and blocking maps side by side.
  • Lookbook Pictures for the look you want: costume, sets, colour, light.
  • Reference Media Your own files and pictures, kept with the film.
  • Songs Each song planned as its own block of shooting.
  • Locations Where you will shoot, with notes from your visits and how to get there.

3 · Pre-Production, the plan

Schedule it and cost it

  • Shoot Calendar When prep starts, when you shoot, when the edit is due.
  • Stripboard Scenes in shooting order, grouped into shoot days.
  • Day Out of Days Which actor works on which day, so nobody is called in for nothing.
  • Sides The script pages each actor needs for the day, ready to print.
  • Budget What the film will cost, line by line, with Chennai rates to start from.
  • Schedule Impact What a shoot day costs, and what cutting scenes really saves. A day is saved only when its last scene goes.
  • Budget Versions Lock the budget as it stands, then compare any later change against it.
  • Cast & Crew Phone and e-mail, by department.
  • Production Reports Printable lists of what each scene and each shoot day needs.

4 · Production & 5 · Post

Shoot it and finish it

  • Shoot Day Today’s scenes on one screen, with a mark for what you shot: shot, part or dropped.
  • Call Sheets The sheet that tells everyone where to be and when, with sunrise and golden hour per location.
  • Edit Log What the edit still needs against what was shot, scene by scene, with a link to each scene’s dailies.
  • Cut Versions Name each cut, from assembly onward, and keep notes against timecodes.
  • Deliverables Censor certificate, subtitles, cinema copy and festival files, checked off.
  • Cloud Workflow An optional checklist for a film whose footage travels through the cloud: offload, backup, folders. Add the link to where each file lives, in Drive, Frame.io, LucidLink or Dropbox. The studio keeps the address, never the files.

      Frequently asked questions

      How is my screenplay kept private?

      Your scripts, characters and breakdowns are saved in this browser’s own storage on your device. Sign in with Google and your films can also sync to the studio’s cloud, where row-level security keeps each account’s rows to itself; connect Google Drive and a backup file sits in your own Drive. Both are optional. The Privacy Policy lists exactly what is collected and why.

      Can I import from Final Draft or Fountain?

      Yes. Bring in a Fountain, Final Draft (.fdx), text or PDF screenplay, and the studio reads the scene headings, action, characters and dialogue into its own format. Export is a properly formatted screenplay PDF. Scanned or encrypted PDFs are refused with a reason rather than guessed at.

      Does it work offline on a film set?

      Yes. The studio installs like an app and caches itself, so you can keep writing, reordering the stripboard and printing call sheets without a signal. Anything you changed offline is still there when you reconnect.

      Are Indian cinema conventions supported?

      Yes. Tamil or Tanglish dialogue with slug lines and action in English, a FEFSI bata table that feeds the budget, CBFC content flags, songs planned as their own blocks of shooting, and Chennai rates that say when they were last checked.

      What do I get in the free trial?

      Every module in the studio, on the Dragon and The Dark Knight sample films, for a limited time — no card and nothing to install. When it ends you choose a plan, and everything you changed is still there.

      Which plan is right for me?

      {{fms:plan.starter}} if you are writing: story, screenplay, drafts and the learning library. {{fms:plan.indie}} if you are going to shoot it: breakdown, stripboard, budget, locations and the paperwork a unit needs. {{fms:plan.pro}} for the whole film, including the shoot day, call sheets, the edit log, deliverables and the AI tools. You can move up later by paying the difference.

      Is it really one payment? No subscription?

      Yes. A plan is bought once with full-time access, and it never renews or lapses. Prices are in Indian rupees, paid through Razorpay, and confirmed at checkout.

      I have never made a film. Is this actually for me?

      It is built for exactly that. The {{fms:short.steps}}-step short blueprint assumes you know nothing and explains each step before it asks you for anything, and the {{fms:glossary.count}} terms in the library are there for the moment somebody says “we’ll pick it up in the edit” and you need to know what that means.

      What if I want my money back?

      You are covered, in full, to the card, UPI, net banking or wallet you paid with, if you were charged twice or by mistake, if you paid and the plan was never delivered (or we activate it, your choice), or wherever Indian law requires a refund. Otherwise a plan is final, which is why the free trial comes first — and on any plan you can export all your work whenever you like. The Refund & Cancellation Policy has the detail and the address to write to.
