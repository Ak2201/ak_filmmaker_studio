Your work, and the fact that it lives in a browser

Everything you write is stored in your browser's local storage. That is deliberate — it is what lets the whole app work offline and with no account. It has one consequence you must take seriously:

  • Clearing your browser's site data for this domain permanently deletes every project on that device.
  • Private or incognito windows discard everything when closed.
  • A browser may evict storage on its own when a device is short of space.
  • A different browser, or a different machine, shows a different, empty studio.

Use the Export button, or connect Drive or an account, and do it before a shoot rather than after one. We cannot recover work that a browser has discarded, because we never had a copy of it.

The reference material is research, not advice

The studio ships rate cards, festival deadlines, craft case studies and budget estimates. They are compiled in good faith and each says where it came from and when it was last checked. They are a starting point for your own enquiries and nothing more.

  • Rates. Only part of the equipment pricing has been verified against a named Chennai vendor; the rest is marked not updated on its own card. Crew rates in particular have no current union floor to quote — the 2022 FEFSI–TFPC wage memorandum expired in March 2025 and has not been replaced. Confirm every figure with the vendor or the crew member before you budget on it.
  • Festival deadlines. Dates move, routes differ, and some festivals publish nothing at all. Each entry records the date it was individually checked. Confirm on the festival's own site before you rely on one.
  • The sample project. The studio ships a sample built around a real released film. The public facts are credited and correct; every piece of production paperwork in it — the schedule, the dates, the budget, the unit list, the call sheets — is invented to give the tools something to show, and says so on its face. It is not a record of how that film was made.
