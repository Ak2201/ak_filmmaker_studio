Your work stays yours

A script is months of work. Here is, in plain words, where it is kept, how you get it out, and who can read it.

  3. A backup in your own Google Drive

download

It comes out in open formats

The screenplay exports as PDF, Word (.docx), Final Draft (.fdx) and Fountain from the Screenplay Writer's File menu. Fountain is plain text and Final Draft files open in most screenwriting programs, so a script written here can be carried on elsewhere.

The breakdown's element index, the shot list, script notes and the budget download as CSV for a spreadsheet, on the plans that include those tools. A backup file holds every project in your studio and restores into any browser from the same menu.

check_circleExport a Fountain file on day one and open it in another app. That is the quickest way to see what travels.

storage

It is saved in your browser first

Every change is written to your own browser's storage on your device before anything else happens. When you are signed in and sync is on, your projects also go to the studio's cloud so they reach your other devices. You can switch sync off in Settings; the copy in your browser stays either way.

That copy is read by the studio itself: it is not a file you can open in another program. Clearing your browser's site data removes it from that device, so keep a backup file or a Drive backup as well.

backup

A backup in your own Google Drive

On Intermediate and Pro the studio can keep a backup file of your studio in your own Google Drive. It uses Google's drive.file permission, which lets it see only the files it created, never anything else in your Drive.

The file belongs to you: you can open, download or delete it from Drive at any time, and disconnecting Drive never deletes it.

cloud_off

It keeps working when the signal drops

The studio installs like an app and caches itself, so you can write, move strips and print call sheets on set without a connection. It keeps opening without a signal for up to 14 days after it last checked your sign-in online on that device; after that it needs a connection once to check your sign-in and plan. Until then your work is hidden, never deleted: it is still in your browser when the check succeeds.

lock

Who can read a script

A synced project can be reached only by you and the people you give access to. The database checks every request against the signed-in account, so a share link you make (to view, comment or edit) is what lets someone else in, and it ends when it expires or you remove it.

Our administrators can see account and payment records, but the console does not show your project content. As the operator of the database we can technically reach synced content; we do not open it unless you ask us to help, you ask for your data, or the law requires it. The privacy policy lists every case.

If the service ever stops

What you keep without us: every file you exported (PDF, Word, Final Draft, Fountain, CSV) and every backup file or Drive backup you made. Those are files on your own device or in your own Drive, and the screenplay formats open in other programs.

What depends on us: signing in, sync between devices, and opening the studio itself, which is also the only way to read the copy in your browser. Without our server the studio opens for at most 14 days after its last online check; after that the browser copy stays on the device but stays hidden. Our terms say that if we ever discontinue the service, we will give members reasonable notice and a way to export their synced data. So keep your own exports: they are the copy no company can take away.
